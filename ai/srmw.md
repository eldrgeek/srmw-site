## The World’s Stupidest Novel

“The World’s Stupidest Novel is the world’s stupidest title for a novel, even if it is the title of the world’s stupidest novel,” says my inner critic.

My inner critic’s yammering voice has echoed in my head for as long as I can remember. It never misses a chance to point out the mistakes  I’m making; to remind me of the mistakes I have made; to predict the mistakes that I will inevitably make; and to make sure I know that nothing that I do will ever, ever be good enough.

“ The World’s Stupidest Novel is not the title I see,” says a reader. Perhaps that reader was you, the person reading this sentence.

“That wasn’t me,” says another reader. “But I don’t see that title either.”

“Me either,” says yet another.

“That not the title anymore,” I explain. “I changed it while I was writing the first draft. That old title was just a placeholder.”

“Self-referential metanovel Writing for D*mmies: a self-referential metanovel?” Asks my inner critic after I change the title. “At least I understood The World’s Stupidest Novel. It was appropriate. Your novel really was stupid. Still is. You need to change it again. No one will understand the new title.”

“The title stays,” I say. “I like it.”

“Well, I don’t like it,”says my inner critic. “And the whole book is stupid. Not just the title.”

“You never like anything, do you?” I ask my inner critic.

“I like criticizing,” says my inner critic.

“Aside from that,” I say. “Have you ever said anything nice about anything that I’ve done?”

“That’s not my job,” says my inner critic. “My job is to criticize. Your job is to do things that I can criticize.

“And I must say, I do my job far better than you do.”

## The Most Powerful Forces In The Universe

Decision is one of the most powerful forces in the universe. Each moment each universe has an infinite number of possible next moments. Each next moment has an infinite number of possible subsequent moments, chained into an infinite number of possible futures. Decisions determine which of the possible next moments becomes the next future present moment. Decisions make the other previously possible next moments impossible and make the potential futures that extend only from no-longer possible next moments impossible. Decisions have the power to change universes. They are powerful—and dangerous.

Imagination is a comparably powerful force. Imagination can create entire new universes and create possible future moments for existing universes. Imagination influences decision; but decision controls imagination. Decide you can’t imagine something and you can’t imagine it. Decide that what you imagine can never be real and you can’t make it real. But decide that what you imagine could be real, then it not only can be real, it becomes real—if only in your imagination.

Nothing can exist and nothing can happen without decision—not even imagination or imagining—and except for evolution, nothing important can happen unless someone, somewhere, imagines it. This makes decision and imagination necessary for most of existence.

Belief is even more powerful than decision or imagination. Most people don’t control what they believe because most people believe they can’t control what they believe. As an inevitable consequence, most people let their beliefs control them.

Once a person who doesn’t control belief believes something, they find it hard to even imagine something that is inconsistent with that belief; they find it hard to decide to imagine something that’s contrary to that belief; and they find it nearly impossible to believe something that’s inconsistent with that existing belief.

Most people find it nearly impossible to change their beliefs, even when their beliefs cause them enormous amounts of pain.

It starts with childhood miseducation. Children are taught to believe without thinking, and come to believe that believing is a state, or a condition and not an activity and a skill. Like every skill, believing can be improved with practice—but to improve believing you must first believe believing can be improved; second, believe you can improve believing; third, decide to improve believing; fourth, learn how to improve believing; and finally, practice improving believing. Deciding and imagining are also skills. People can improve deciding and imagining—but only if they believe, decide, learn, and practice.

Through my story I discovered my safe place and its importance. Now I go there regularly— in my imagination, at least—and I go whenever I need to. My regular visits help me practice getting there, and since I’m imagining that I’m there, my visits help me practice imagining.

Here’s one way I practice. I set an alarm on my Android that goes off periodically—right now, every fifteen minutes. When the alarm goes off, the phone vibrates, and I start my session.

I stop what I am doing, shut off and reset the alarm. If possible, I stand up. That’s a good practice right there, because I spend a lot of time sitting. If I can, I stretch while I’m doing the rest.

First, I imagine I’m in my safe place, the one that the character who was me in the metanovel found. It takes only a second to do that now, because I’ve practiced a lot. Once in a while I devote more than a second, working to making it realer than usual.
